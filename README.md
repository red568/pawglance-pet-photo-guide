# pawglance-pet-photo-guide

**How to photograph your pet's symptoms for a vet — plus a pre-vet-visit checklist.**

A free, open checklist for dog and cat owners. Good photos and clear notes help your veterinarian understand what changed, when, and how fast. This guide covers what to shoot, how to shoot it, and what to write down before the appointment.

> ⚠️ **Disclaimer:** This guide is for general education only. It is not veterinary advice and does not diagnose any condition. If your pet has trouble breathing, collapses, has a seizure, is bleeding heavily, may have eaten something toxic, or seems in severe pain, contact a veterinarian or emergency clinic right away. Do not wait to take photos.

## Why photos help

Many problems come and go: a limp that disappears in the clinic, a rash that fades, an eye that is only red in the morning, vomit that has been cleaned up. A clear, dated photo shows your vet what you saw at home. A series of photos shows how it changed over time.

## General photo rules

1. **Use natural, even light.** Near a window is best. Avoid harsh flash, which washes out redness and color.
2. **Get two shots: one close-up and one wider.** The close-up shows detail; the wider shot shows where on the body it is.
3. **Add scale.** Place a coin, ruler, or your fingertip next to lumps, wounds, and spots (only if your pet is calm).
4. **Hold steady and focus.** Tap the screen on the area to focus. Take several shots and keep the sharpest.
5. **Keep the same angle over time.** Follow-up photos are most useful when they match the first one.
6. **Check the date.** Make sure your phone keeps the date and time on each photo.
7. **Short video for movement.** Limping, coughing, head shaking, breathing effort, and scratching are better captured in a 10–20 second video.
8. **Stay safe.** Never restrain a painful or frightened animal to get a photo. A blurry photo is better than a bite.

## Body area guides

| Area | What to capture | Tips |
|---|---|---|
| **Eyes** | Both eyes, straight on; then each eye close up | Compare left vs right. Note discharge color, squinting, cloudiness, redness of the white. |
| **Ears** | Ear flap (inside and out) and the opening of the canal | Don't push anything into the ear. Note smell, head shaking, scratching. |
| **Skin & coat** | The spot close up + wider shot of the area; part the fur | Note itching, licking, hair loss, scabs, dandruff, flea dirt. |
| **Lumps & bumps** | Lump with a coin or ruler next to it, from top and side | Note size, whether it is soft/firm, moves under the skin, and when you first found it. |
| **Wounds** | Wound close up + wider shot; before and after cleaning if safe | Note bleeding, swelling, discharge, smell. |
| **Mouth, teeth & gums** | Lift the lip gently on each side | Note gum color, tartar, broken teeth, bad breath, drooling. |
| **Paws & nails** | Top, pads, and between toes | Note licking, limping, redness, swelling, broken nails. |
| **Vomit, poop & urine** | Photo before cleaning up, with something for scale | Note color, consistency, blood, mucus, foreign material, frequency. |
| **Body condition** | Top-down view and side view, standing | Useful for weight concerns. Note appetite and recent weight changes. |

## What to write down

Keep a short note with your photos. Your vet will likely ask:

- **When did it start?** Date and time you first noticed it.
- **Is it getting better, worse, or the same?**
- **How often?** (for vomiting, diarrhea, coughing, scratching, etc.)
- **Eating, drinking, peeing, pooping:** any changes?
- **Energy and behavior:** hiding, restless, less playful, more sleepy?
- **Anything new?** Food, treats, medications, flea/tick products, cleaning products, plants, travel, boarding, new pets.
- **What have you already tried?** Including any home remedies or medicines given, with doses and times.
- **Pet basics:** species, breed, age, weight, spayed/neutered, current medications, known conditions.

## Pre-vet-visit checklist

Copy this into your notes app before the appointment:

```markdown
- [ ] Photos and videos saved in one album, sorted by date
- [ ] Short timeline: when it started, how it changed
- [ ] List of current medications, supplements, and flea/tick/heartworm products
- [ ] Food brand and any recent diet changes
- [ ] Changes in appetite, thirst, urination, stool, energy
- [ ] Anything already tried at home (what, when, how much)
- [ ] Previous records or test results, if seeing a new clinic
- [ ] Stool or urine sample, if your clinic asked for one
- [ ] Carrier or leash, and treats for a calmer visit
- [ ] Your list of questions (see below)
```

## Questions to ask your vet

- What do you think is most likely, and what else are you ruling out?
- Do we need any tests? What will each one tell us?
- What signs at home mean I should come back sooner or go to an emergency clinic?
- How should I give this medication, and what side effects should I watch for?
- When should we recheck, and what should I photograph before then?
- Is there anything I should stop doing or avoid?
- What will this cost, and are there options if budget is a concern?

## Tools

You can keep photos in any album or notes app. If you want help structuring what you see, [PawGlance](https://pawglance.com/) is an AI pet photo tool for dogs and cats that turns a single photo into organized observations, a list of what the photo cannot show, and questions for your vet. It is educational and does not provide a diagnosis. You can view an example at https://pawglance.com/sample-report.

Other useful habits:

- A shared family note so everyone logs the same symptoms.
- A weekly photo of any chronic issue (skin spot, lump, eye) taken in the same place and light.
